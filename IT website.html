System 01

Anti-Lock
Braking System

ABS prevents wheels from locking during hard braking, maintaining steering control and reducing stopping distances on slippery surfaces.

  • How It Works: Wheel speed sensors monitor each wheel. If a wheel begins to lock, the ECU rapidly modulates brake pressure — releasing and reapplying up to 15 times per second.
  • Steering Control: By preventing lock-up, ABS allows the driver to steer around obstacles even during maximum braking — something impossible with locked wheels.
  • Threshold Braking: ABS keeps each wheel at the optimal slip ratio (~10–20%) where longitudinal grip is maximized for the shortest stopping distance.
  • Sensor Technology: Each wheel has a toothed ring and magnetic sensor. The ECU detects deceleration rates exceeding a threshold and triggers the hydraulic modulator.
  • Pedal Feedback: Drivers feel a pulsing vibration through the brake pedal — this is normal and means ABS is working. Maintain firm, steady pressure.

System 02

Airbag
Systems

Supplemental Restraint Systems (SRS) deploy a cushion between the occupant and hard surfaces within milliseconds of impact detection.

Frontal Airbags

Located in the steering wheel and dashboard. Deploy in head-on collisions above ~25 km/h, cushioning the driver and front passenger from the steering column and windshield.

30–50ms deployment

Side Curtain Airbags

Deploy from the roofline downward to cover side windows. Critical in T-bone collisions and rollovers, preventing head contact with door pillars and ejection through windows.

15–25ms deployment

Knee & Torso Airbags

Knee airbags reduce leg fractures by distributing loads across the lower extremities. Torso/seat airbags protect the thorax and abdomen in side impacts from door intrusion.

20–40ms deployment

Deployment Sequence

0 ms

Impact Detected

Accelerometers and crash sensors detect rapid deceleration. The ACM (Airbag Control Module) evaluates crash severity.

8–15 ms

Deployment Signal

ACM sends electrical signal to the inflator. Squib (initiator) ignites the propellant charge inside the module.

20–40 ms

Full Inflation

Nitrogen gas fills the nylon bag completely. The occupant contacts the fully inflated cushion, spreading impact energy.

60–80 ms

Controlled Deflation

Vent holes allow controlled deflation, preventing rebound injury. The entire event concludes in under the blink of an eye.

Key Safety Facts

  • Airbags reduce driver fatality risk in frontal crashes by approximately 29% when combined with a seatbelt.
  • The gas used is nitrogen, generated by the rapid combustion of sodium azide or similar propellants — a completely controlled chemical reaction.
  • Modern vehicles have 6–10 airbags as standard. Some luxury vehicles include up to 12, including far-side and center airbags.
  • Always wear your seatbelt: airbags are a supplemental system and can cause injury if the occupant is unbelted and too close to the module.
System 03

Braking
Systems

Understanding the physics and components of automotive braking helps explain why modern stopping performance is so dramatically improved over older vehicles.

Disc Brakes

Cast iron or carbon ceramic rotors are clamped by hydraulic callipers containing brake pads. Friction converts kinetic energy to heat. Ventilated rotors dissipate heat faster, preventing brake fade under repeated heavy use.

Drum Brakes

Found on some rear axles, curved shoes press outward against a rotating drum. Less effective heat dissipation but lower cost, still adequate for the lighter braking loads on rear wheels.

Hydraulic System

Pressing the pedal pushes a piston in the master cylinder, sending pressurised brake fluid through steel lines and flexible hoses to each wheel's caliper or wheel cylinder — multiplying your foot force by 30–40×.

  • Brake Bias: More braking force is sent to the front wheels (typically 70/30 split) because weight transfers forward under deceleration, increasing front tyre grip.
  • Emergency Brake Assist (EBA): Detects panic braking from rapid pedal application and automatically applies maximum brake pressure — many drivers don't press hard enough in emergencies.
  • Electronic Brake-force Distribution (EBD): Works with ABS to dynamically adjust front/rear brake bias based on vehicle load and road conditions.
  • Regenerative Braking (EVs): Electric motors act as generators during deceleration, recovering kinetic energy as electricity. Physical brakes blend in as needed for stronger stops.
  • Maintenance Matters: Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, lowering its boiling point. Flush every 2 years. Pads typically last 50,000–70,000 km but should be inspected regularly.
